# Disciplinary Process Guide

For managers handling staff concerns in a care setting.

This guide follows the handbook-style route: deal with concerns early, keep clear records, use support where appropriate, investigate fairly, hold a formal meeting where needed, give a proportionate outcome, and offer an appeal.

It should be read alongside the staff handbook, ACAS guidance, CQC expectations, and Skills for Care guidance on supervision, safer employment, and identifying concerns.

## 1. First decision: what type of issue is it?

Before taking action, decide what you are dealing with.

| Issue type | Meaning | Usual route |
|---|---|---|
| Welfare | Health, stress, personal difficulty, or support need | Welfare meeting, support plan, review |
| Conduct | Behaviour, attitude, attendance, records, or rule breach | Informal action, matter of concern, or disciplinary route |
| Capability | Skill, competence, training, or performance gap | Capability route, support, training, review |
| Safeguarding | Risk to a resident, dignity, safety, abuse, neglect, or poor practice | Safeguarding response, investigation, possible formal action |
| Whistleblowing | Protected disclosure or concern raised about wrongdoing | Whistleblowing route, not retaliation or discipline |
| Grievance | Employee complaint about treatment or workplace issue | Grievance route |

Do not force every issue into disciplinary action. Use the right route.

## 2. Record of conversation

A record of conversation is an early management record. It is used when a concern is minor, early, unclear, or supportable.

It is not a formal disciplinary sanction.

Use it for:
- low-level conduct concerns
- first-time lateness or absence concerns
- minor documentation issues
- attitude or communication concerns
- reminder of standards
- welfare or support discussions
- early supervision follow-up

The record should include:

| Point | What to record |
|---|---|
| Date and time | When the conversation happened |
| People present | Manager and staff member |
| Concern | Facts only, not opinion |
| Staff response | What the employee said |
| Support offered | Training, supervision, adjustment, guidance, welfare support |
| Expected standard | What needs to happen next |
| Review date | When this will be checked |
| Manager note | Whether the issue may escalate if repeated |

Suggested wording:

"This is a record of our conversation. It is not a formal disciplinary warning. It records the concern discussed, the standard expected, any support agreed, and the review date. If the concern continues or becomes more serious, the matter may be considered under the formal process."

## 3. Supervision

Supervision is a regular recorded meeting or discussion between a manager and staff member.

It should review:
- work practice
- conduct
- performance
- wellbeing
- learning and competence
- safeguarding awareness
- support needs
- agreed actions

Skills for Care supports regular supervision for staff, with closer supervision where risk has been identified. In a care service, supervision is part of safe management and evidence of support.

Supervision can identify concerns, set actions, and show what support was offered. It is not a disciplinary sanction.

Escalate from supervision where:
- the same concern keeps repeating
- the concern affects resident safety or dignity
- records, medication, or safeguarding are involved
- the employee does not engage with agreed actions
- the issue appears to be misconduct, not a support need

## 4. Matter of concern

A matter of concern is used when the issue needs closer attention before deciding whether formal action is needed.

Use it when:
- facts are incomplete
- there is a repeated pattern
- informal support has not worked
- the issue may be conduct or capability
- care records, handover, medication, attendance, or conduct are affected
- there is a low-level concern that needs recording and review

A matter of concern should not decide guilt. It should identify the concern and decide the next step.

Possible outcomes:

| Outcome | When used |
|---|---|
| No further action | Concern not supported by facts |
| Informal support | Minor issue, support likely to resolve it |
| Supervision review | Issue needs monitoring |
| Capability route | Skill, training, or competence issue |
| Investigation | More facts needed or formal action may be needed |
| Safeguarding escalation | Risk to resident safety, dignity, or welfare |

## 5. Fact-finding

Fact-finding is a short evidence-gathering stage. It helps the manager decide whether the issue should be closed, managed informally, or investigated formally.

Use fact-finding to check:
- what happened
- when it happened
- who was involved
- what records exist
- whether there is an immediate risk
- whether the employee has an initial explanation
- whether safeguarding or CQC reporting needs checking

Evidence may include:
- care notes
- medication records
- handover records
- rota and attendance records
- incident forms
- supervision notes
- witness accounts
- training and competency records
- CCTV, where lawful and relevant

Do not treat fact-finding as a disciplinary hearing.

## 6. Investigation

Move to investigation when the issue may justify formal disciplinary action or where the facts are serious or disputed.

An investigation should be:
- prompt
- impartial
- evidence-based
- proportionate to the seriousness of the issue
- clearly recorded

The investigator should look at both sides. They should not decide the outcome before the hearing.

The investigation should answer:

| Question | Reason |
|---|---|
| What is the allegation or concern? | Keeps the process clear |
| What evidence supports it? | Shows the factual basis |
| What evidence does not support it? | Keeps the process fair |
| What does the employee say? | Gives a right to respond |
| Is this conduct, capability, welfare, or safeguarding? | Uses the right route |
| Is formal action proportionate? | Avoids over-escalation |

In care settings, also ask:
- Did this affect resident safety or dignity?
- Was there a safeguarding issue?
- Were care notes, medication records, or incident records affected?
- Did supervision or training identify the risk earlier?
- Was the issue reported and escalated correctly?
- Is there a repeated pattern?

## 7. Suspension

Suspension should not be automatic.

Only consider suspension where it is needed to:
- protect residents, staff, or the employee
- protect evidence
- allow a fair investigation
- manage a serious safeguarding or safety risk

Suspension is not a punishment. Keep it under review and confirm it in writing.

Consider alternatives first, such as:
- temporary redeployment
- removing specific duties
- increased supervision
- shift change
- restricted access to records or medication tasks

## 8. Formal disciplinary meeting

Hold a formal disciplinary meeting where the investigation suggests there is a case to answer.

Before the meeting, give the employee:
- written notice of the meeting
- the allegation or concern
- copies of relevant evidence
- possible outcomes
- the right to be accompanied, where applicable
- enough time to prepare

At the meeting:
- explain the concern
- go through the evidence
- let the employee respond
- ask fair questions
- consider mitigation
- adjourn before deciding

Do not decide the outcome before the meeting.

## 9. Outcomes

The outcome must be proportionate to the facts, the handbook, the employee's response, and the risk involved.

| Outcome | When it may fit |
|---|---|
| No action | Concern not proven or no formal action justified |
| Informal action | Minor issue, support or reminder is enough |
| First written warning | Lower-level misconduct or repeated concern |
| Final written warning | Serious concern or repeated misconduct with continued risk |
| Dismissal with notice | Serious or repeated misconduct where dismissal is fair and justified |
| Summary dismissal | Gross misconduct where dismissal without notice is justified after a fair process |

Outcome letters should state:
- the concern considered
- the decision
- the reason for the decision
- any warning length
- improvement required
- review date
- consequences if the issue repeats
- appeal route and deadline

## 10. Gross misconduct and serious concerns

The staff handbook approach treats some issues as potentially serious because they can damage trust, safety, records, or legal compliance.

Examples in a care setting include:
- falsifying care records
- serious medication breach
- safeguarding breach
- abuse, neglect, or poor treatment of a resident
- attending work impaired by alcohol or drugs
- unauthorised removal of records, property, or data
- serious dishonesty
- serious breach of confidentiality
- violence, threat, or serious misconduct towards residents, staff, or visitors

Gross misconduct may justify summary dismissal, but only after a fair process unless there is a lawful reason why that cannot happen.

## 11. Appeal

Offer an appeal against formal outcomes.

The appeal should be heard by someone appropriate and, where possible, not involved in the original decision.

The appeal should consider:
- whether the process was fair
- whether the facts supported the decision
- whether the outcome was proportionate
- whether new evidence changes the position
- whether the employee was given a fair chance to respond

Confirm the appeal outcome in writing.

## 12. Closure and follow-up

After the process, close the loop.

Check:
- outcome letter issued
- records stored securely
- appeal period noted
- supervision or review dates set
- safeguarding or CQC actions completed where needed
- training or competence actions arranged
- rota, medication, or record access controls updated where needed

## Escalation route

| Stage | Threshold | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Record of conversation | Minor, early, or supportable issue | Discuss, record, agree standard and review |
| Supervision | Routine management or repeated low-level issue | Support, monitor, agree actions |
| Matter of concern | Pattern, unclear facts, or issue needs closer review | Fact-find and decide route |
| Investigation | Formal action may be needed or facts are serious/disputed | Gather evidence fairly |
| Disciplinary meeting | Case to answer | Hear employee response |
| Formal outcome | Misconduct proven or formal correction needed | Warning, final warning, dismissal, or no action |
| Appeal | Employee challenges formal outcome | Independent review |

## Manager checklist

- Is the concern factual and recorded?
- Have welfare and support been considered?
- Is this conduct, capability, safeguarding, grievance, or whistleblowing?
- Is resident safety or dignity affected?
- Have care records, medication records, incident forms, and supervision notes been checked?
- Has the employee had a fair chance to respond?
- Is formal action proportionate?
- Has the appeal route been explained?
