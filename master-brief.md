# Master brief — HR disciplinary process

## Purpose
This pack covers the full disciplinary journey in UK workplaces, from welfare meeting and matters of concern through investigation, formal hearing, warnings, final warning, appeal, and termination.

## Executive summary
A fair disciplinary process starts with early, proportionate support where possible. If the issue is minor or developmental, a welfare meeting or informal coaching may be enough. If concerns persist or the matter is more serious, the employer should move to fact-finding, investigation, and a formal meeting. The employee should be told the allegation, given an opportunity to respond, and allowed to appeal the outcome where appropriate.

Warnings should be clear, time-bound, and proportionate. Final warnings and dismissal should only follow a fair process and should be justified by the facts, policy, and applicable law. In UK practice, the Acas Code of Practice is the key benchmark for fairness.

## Core principles
- fairness and consistency
- prompt, impartial investigation
- clear written notice
- right to respond
- right to be accompanied where applicable
- proportionate outcomes
- appeal
- record keeping

## Typical flow
1. Welfare meeting / informal support
2. Matters of concern / fact-finding
3. Investigation
4. Formal disciplinary meeting
5. Outcome: no action, warning, final warning, or dismissal
6. Appeal
7. Closure

## Care-sector emphasis
In care settings, disciplinary action should be handled with extra attention to:
- safeguarding
- documentation
- medication safety
- supervision and competence
- CQC / regulatory reporting duties
- resident safety

## Best source layers
1. Acas Code and Acas guide
2. GOV.UK disciplinary and dismissal guidance
3. CIPD disciplinary factsheet
4. CQC and Skills for Care for care-sector context
