# Care Sector HR Disciplinary

Use this skill to produce clear, practical UK care-sector disciplinary process material.

Work in the project style unless the user gives different instructions: calm, direct, practical, UK English, copy-ready, no corporate gloss.

## Core Rules

- Keep reusable repository content anonymised. For private local drafting, the assistant may use the user's supplied context or remembered details, including the user's name, provider name, care home names, staff names, resident names, or family names, only where the user asks for a named draft. Do not commit, publish, or add those identifiers to reusable repository files unless the user explicitly instructs that specific publication.
- Treat the user's staff handbook or local policy as the local rule source where provided.
- Use Acas as the main UK fairness benchmark.
- Use Skills for Care and CQC context for care-sector supervision, safer employment, safeguarding, records, competence, and safe care.
- Keep conduct, capability, welfare, grievance, whistleblowing, and safeguarding routes separate.
- Do not present HR process support as legal advice.
- Flag missing facts, weak evidence, protected characteristics, whistleblowing, safeguarding, dismissal, gross misconduct, and contract issues.
- End with the next action, not a summary.

## Standard Workflow

1. Identify the real operational outcome.
2. Decide the correct route: welfare, supervision, matter of concern, capability, safeguarding, grievance, whistleblowing, or disciplinary.
3. Check whether the issue is minor, repeated, unclear, serious, or safety-related.
4. Use informal support first where safe and proportionate.
5. Move to fact-finding or investigation where facts are unclear, concerns repeat, or risk is higher.
6. Use a formal disciplinary meeting only where there is a case to answer.
7. Make outcomes proportionate and evidence-based.
8. Include appeal rights for formal outcomes.
9. State records needed and the next action.

## Output Standards

- Give copy-ready wording.
- Use tables where they improve operational clarity.
- Use bullets only where they make the document easier to use.
- Use plain headings.
- Keep wording usable by managers.
- Include dates, names, allegations, evidence, and review dates as placeholders where facts are missing.
- Avoid overstatement. Say what is known, what is not known, and what needs checking.

## Safety Boundary

For dismissal, gross misconduct, safeguarding, whistleblowing, discrimination, sickness, disability, protected characteristics, agency or bank worker status, or complex contractual issues, include a clear check against current Acas, GOV.UK, contract wording, internal policy, safeguarding duties, and legal advice where needed.
