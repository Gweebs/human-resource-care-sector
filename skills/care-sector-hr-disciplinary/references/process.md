# Process Reference

Use this when the user asks for the full process, escalation thresholds, manager guidance, decision trees, or disciplinary process wording.

## Main Flow

1. Welfare meeting or informal support
2. Record of conversation
3. Supervision
4. Matter of concern
5. Fact-finding
6. Investigation
7. Formal disciplinary meeting
8. Outcome
9. Appeal
10. Closure and follow-up

## First Decision

| Issue type | Meaning | Usual route |
|---|---|---|
| Welfare | Health, stress, personal difficulty, or support need | Welfare meeting, support plan, review |
| Conduct | Actions, behaviour, attitude, attendance, honesty, record keeping, or rule-following | Informal action, matter of concern, or disciplinary route |
| Capability | Skill, competence, training, confidence, or health capacity issue | Capability route, support, training, review |
| Safeguarding | Risk to a resident, dignity, safety, abuse, neglect, or poor practice | Safeguarding response, investigation, possible formal action |
| Whistleblowing | Protected disclosure or concern raised about wrongdoing | Whistleblowing route |
| Grievance | Employee complaint about treatment or workplace issue | Grievance route |

Use this test:

Did the employee know what was expected and choose not to follow it, or did they lack the ability, training, health, or support to meet the standard?

If it is choice, refusal, dishonesty, unsafe action, poor attitude, or rule breach, it is usually conduct.

If it is a skills gap, training need, health issue, or genuine inability, it is usually capability or welfare first.

## Record Of Conversation

A record of conversation is an early management record. It is not a formal disciplinary sanction.

Use it for:
- low-level conduct concerns
- first-time lateness or absence concerns
- minor documentation issues
- attitude or communication concerns
- reminders of standards
- welfare or support discussions
- early supervision follow-up

Record:
- date and time
- people present
- factual concern
- employee response
- support offered
- expected standard
- review date
- whether the matter may escalate if repeated

Suggested wording:

"This is a record of our conversation. It is not a formal disciplinary warning. It records the concern discussed, the standard expected, any support agreed, and the review date. If the concern continues or becomes more serious, the matter may be considered under the formal process."

## Matter Of Concern

A matter of concern is used when an issue needs closer attention before deciding whether formal action is needed.

Use it when:
- facts are incomplete
- there is a repeated pattern
- informal support has not worked
- the issue may be conduct or capability
- care records, handover, medication, attendance, or conduct are affected
- a low-level concern needs recording and review

Possible outcomes:

| Outcome | When used |
|---|---|
| No further action | Concern not supported by facts |
| Informal support | Minor issue, support likely to resolve it |
| Supervision review | Issue needs monitoring |
| Capability route | Skill, training, or competence issue |
| Investigation | More facts needed or formal action may be needed |
| Safeguarding escalation | Risk to resident safety, dignity, or welfare |

## Fact-Finding

Fact-finding is a short evidence-gathering stage. It is not a disciplinary hearing.

Check:
- what happened
- when it happened
- who was involved
- what records exist
- whether there is immediate risk
- whether the employee has an initial explanation
- whether safeguarding or CQC reporting needs checking

## Investigation

Move to investigation when formal disciplinary action may be needed or facts are serious or disputed.

The investigation should be:
- prompt
- impartial
- evidence-based
- proportionate
- clearly recorded

The investigator should not decide the outcome before the hearing.

Investigation questions:
- What is the allegation or concern?
- What evidence supports it?
- What evidence does not support it?
- What does the employee say?
- Is this conduct, capability, welfare, or safeguarding?
- Is formal action proportionate?

## Suspension

Suspension should not be automatic. It is not a punishment.

Consider suspension only where needed to:
- protect residents, staff, or the employee
- protect evidence
- allow a fair investigation
- manage serious safeguarding or safety risk

Consider alternatives first:
- temporary redeployment
- removing specific duties
- increased supervision
- shift change
- restricted access to records or medication tasks

## Formal Disciplinary Meeting

Before the meeting, give the employee:
- written notice
- allegation or concern
- relevant evidence
- possible outcomes
- right to be accompanied, where applicable
- enough time to prepare

At the meeting:
- explain the concern
- go through evidence
- let the employee respond
- ask fair questions
- consider mitigation
- adjourn before deciding

## Outcomes

| Outcome | When it may fit |
|---|---|
| No action | Concern not proven or no formal action justified |
| Informal action | Minor issue, support or reminder is enough |
| First written warning | Lower-level misconduct or repeated concern |
| Final written warning | Serious concern or repeated misconduct with continued risk |
| Dismissal with notice | Serious or repeated misconduct where dismissal is fair and justified |
| Summary dismissal | Gross misconduct where dismissal without notice is justified after a fair process |

Outcome letters should include:
- concern considered
- decision
- reason for decision
- warning length, if applicable
- improvement required
- review date
- consequences if repeated
- appeal route and deadline

## Appeal

Offer appeal against formal outcomes.

The appeal should consider:
- whether the process was fair
- whether facts supported the decision
- whether outcome was proportionate
- whether new evidence changes the position
- whether the employee had a fair chance to respond

Confirm the appeal outcome in writing.
