# Templates And Outputs Reference

Use this when drafting documents, letters, guides, forms, Figma-ready content, or manager tools.

## General Drafting Rules

- Make it copy-ready.
- Use UK English.
- Use plain headings.
- Use tables for decisions, thresholds, evidence, and action plans.
- Keep wording manager-friendly.
- Include placeholders for missing facts.
- Avoid long legal explanation unless needed.
- End with the next action.

If the concern continues or becomes more serious, the matter may be considered under the formal process."

## Disciplinary Invite Letter Structure

Include:
- date
- employee name
- meeting date, time, and place
- allegation or concern
- possible outcomes
- evidence enclosed
- right to be accompanied
- who will chair
- who will take notes
- request for evidence or witnesses
- contact for adjustments or support

## Outcome Letter Structure

Include:
- concern considered
- evidence reviewed
- employee response
- finding
- outcome
- reason
- improvement required
- timescale or warning duration
- review date
- consequences if repeated
- appeal route and deadline

## Appeal Acknowledgement Structure

Include:
- appeal received
- grounds of appeal
- appeal meeting details
- who will hear the appeal
- right to be accompanied
- evidence or documents needed
- next step

## Figma-Ready Guide Structure

Suggested frames:

1. Purpose and principles
2. First decision: what type of issue is it?
3. Record of conversation
4. Supervision
5. Matter of concern
6. Fact-finding
7. Investigation
8. Suspension
9. Formal disciplinary meeting
10. Outcomes
11. Gross misconduct
12. Appeal
13. Closure and follow-up
14. Escalation route
15. Manager checklist

Suggested flow:

Record of conversation > Supervision > Matter of concern > Fact-finding > Investigation > Disciplinary meeting > Outcome > Appeal > Closure

Safety, safeguarding, medication, falsified records, or serious conduct can move the process straight to investigation, with suspension considered only where necessary.
