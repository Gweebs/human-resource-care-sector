# Care-Sector Reference

Use this for care homes, domiciliary care, supported living, and other regulated adult social care settings.

## Care-Sector Emphasis

Care-sector disciplinary work must balance employee fairness with resident safety.

Give extra attention to:
- safeguarding
- resident safety and dignity
- medication handling
- accurate care records
- supervision and competence
- incident reporting
- CQC or regulatory duties
- Skills for Care guidance on supervision, safer employment, and identifying concerns

## Supervision

Supervision is a regular recorded meeting or discussion between a manager and staff member.

It reviews:
- work practice
- conduct
- performance
- wellbeing
- learning and competence
- safeguarding awareness
- support needs
- agreed actions

Supervision can identify concerns, set actions, and show support offered. It is not a disciplinary sanction.

Skills for Care supports regular supervision for staff, with closer supervision where risk has been identified. Safer employment guidance recommends regular supervision for all staff, at least once a month, with extra supervision where recruitment or vetting risk has been identified.

## Low-Level Concerns

Record low-level concerns and repeated patterns early.

Escalate from supervision where:
- the same concern keeps repeating
- resident safety or dignity is affected
- records, medication, or safeguarding are involved
- the employee does not engage with agreed actions
- the issue appears to be misconduct, not a support need

## Care-Sector Evidence Sources

Use relevant evidence:
- incident reports
- care notes and daily records
- medication administration records
- supervision records
- training and competency records
- witness statements
- CCTV where lawful and relevant
- safeguarding logs
- shift rotas and staffing records

## Care-Sector Investigation Questions

Ask:
- Did the issue affect resident safety or dignity?
- Was there a safeguarding implication?
- Were care notes, medication records, or incident records affected?
- Did supervision or training identify the risk earlier?
- Was the staff member competent for the task?
- Was the incident report and escalation handled correctly?
- Were there repeated conduct, behaviour, or performance concerns before the incident?

## Typical Scenarios

| Scenario | Likely route |
|---|---|
| Repeated lateness affecting handover | Start informal, escalate if repeated |
| Missed documentation with no immediate harm | Matter of concern, check training, workload, and conduct |
| Medication documentation error | Check incident reports, competence, supervision, and reporting duties |
| Safeguarding lapse | Immediate assessment, prompt investigation, possible suspension if necessary |
| Falsified care records | Serious misconduct consideration |
| Alcohol or drug impairment at work | Immediate safety response, fact-finding, likely formal route |
| Unauthorised removal of records or property | Fact-finding, possible serious misconduct |
| Skill gap with new system | Capability route, training, support, review |

## Escalation In Care Settings

Escalate faster where the concern involves:
- safeguarding
- resident safety or dignity
- medication
- falsified records
- serious confidentiality breach
- abuse, neglect, or poor treatment
- impairment at work
- repeated conduct concerns after support

Still keep the process fair, evidence-based, and proportionate.
