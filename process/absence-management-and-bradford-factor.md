# Absence management and Bradford Factor

Use the Bradford Factor as an absence review prompt, not as a disciplinary trigger.

## Purpose

The Bradford Factor can help managers spot frequent short-term absence patterns. It should not decide the outcome.

In a care setting, short-notice absence can affect:

- safe staffing
- handover
- medication rounds
- continuity of care
- resident dignity
- pressure on other staff

The score helps identify when a manager should hold a recorded absence review. The review must still consider the employee's explanation, wellbeing, support needs, equality risks, and the needs of the service.

## Formula

`S x S x D`

| Item | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `S` | Number of separate absence spells in the review period |
| `D` | Total days absent in the same review period |
| Example | 4 separate absences totalling 6 days = `4 x 4 x 6 = 96` |

The review period should be set out in the absence policy. A rolling 52-week period is commonly used, but the provider should check its own policy wording before using it.

## How To Use It

| Stage | Use |
|---|---|
| Welfare meeting | Use the score to prompt a wellbeing and support conversation. |
| Record of conversation | Record the attendance concern, employee explanation, support offered, expected reporting standard, and review date. |
| Supervision | Monitor attendance pattern, stress, health, rota fit, caring responsibilities, and work-related causes. |
| Matter of concern | Use where absence keeps repeating and facts need checking before any formal route. |
| Fact-finding | Use if absence may involve false reporting, failure to follow absence procedure, or unexplained patterns. |
| Disciplinary route | Use only where evidence suggests misconduct, such as dishonest reporting, unauthorised absence, or failure to follow clear absence rules. |

## Manager Checks Before Any Action

Before deciding next steps, the manager should check:

- the absence dates and spells are accurate
- the employee has had a chance to explain the absence
- the absence may be disability-related
- the absence may be pregnancy-related
- the absence may be linked to mental health
- the absence may be caused by work, including stress, injury, rota pressure, or workload
- the absence may relate to dependants or caring responsibilities
- reasonable adjustments may be needed
- occupational health advice may be useful
- flexible working or temporary changes may support attendance
- the employee followed the absence reporting procedure
- the same standard has been applied consistently to other employees

If the facts are unclear, use fact-finding. Do not jump to an outcome.

## Recommended Policy Wording

The Bradford Factor may be used as a review tool to identify repeated short-term absence patterns. Reaching a review score does not automatically lead to disciplinary action. It prompts a recorded absence review to understand the reason for absence, consider support, check whether the absence may be disability-related, pregnancy-related, mental-health-related, work-related, or linked to caring responsibilities, and decide whether any further action is fair and proportionate.

Where the concern appears to be health, welfare, disability, stress, pregnancy, or caring responsibilities, the manager should consider support, reasonable adjustments, occupational health advice, flexible working, or a review plan. Where the concern appears to involve unauthorised absence, false reporting, failure to follow absence reporting rules, or another conduct issue, the manager may consider fact-finding before deciding whether formal action is appropriate.

## Risk Flags

Take advice before formal action where the absence may involve:

- disability
- pregnancy
- mental health
- work-related stress or injury
- protected characteristics
- whistleblowing or grievance
- long-term health condition
- complex medical evidence
- dismissal risk

This is HR process and drafting support, not legal advice. Before dismissal, action involving protected characteristics, or complex sickness absence decisions, check current Acas, GOV.UK, internal policy, contract wording, occupational health evidence, and legal advice where needed.

## Source Notes

Acas states that absence review points should not automatically trigger disciplinary action. They should start an absence review, with consideration of wellbeing, support, disability, pregnancy, mental health, work-related causes, dependants, and flexible working.

GOV.UK states that employers must make reasonable adjustments so disabled workers or workers with physical or mental health conditions are not substantially disadvantaged at work.

Skills for Care guidance supports regular supervision, early recording of concerns, and clear records where conduct, safeguarding, or behaviour concerns arise.
