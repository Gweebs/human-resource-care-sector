# Disciplinary process map

## 1. Welfare meeting / informal support
Purpose:
- identify issues early
- support the employee
- clarify expectations
- avoid escalation where possible

Best practice:
- keep the tone supportive
- record concerns factually
- explain expected standards
- agree review dates and support actions

## 2. Matters of concern / fact-finding
Purpose:
- understand the issue
- determine whether there is enough to move to formal process

Best practice:
- gather facts promptly
- avoid predetermining outcome
- separate allegation from finding
- keep notes

## 3. Investigation
Purpose:
- establish whether formal disciplinary action is warranted

Best practice:
- appoint an appropriate investigator
- gather evidence impartially
- interview relevant parties
- provide the employee a chance to respond

## 4. Formal disciplinary meeting
Purpose:
- consider evidence and the employee’s explanation

Best practice:
- give written notice
- allow accompaniment where required
- share evidence in advance where possible
- confirm the decision in writing

## 5. Warnings
Possible outcomes:
- no action
- informal coaching
- first written warning
- final written warning
- dismissal in serious cases

Best practice:
- warnings should state the issue, improvement required, timescale, and consequences
- final warnings should be time-bound and clear

## 6. Appeal
Purpose:
- give the employee a route to challenge the decision

Best practice:
- hear the appeal promptly
- use an impartial manager
- review both process and substance

## 7. Termination / dismissal
Purpose:
- end employment where conduct, capability, or some other substantial reason justifies it

Best practice:
- only dismiss after a fair process
- consider alternatives
- document reasons carefully
- ensure notice/contractual obligations are handled correctly
