Key points for this disciplinary pack:
- Supervision is a regular process where a manager meets and interacts with staff to review work, provide support, set standards, monitor performance, identify learning needs, and share relevant organisational information.
- Regulated providers should offer regular supervision so staff are competent and confident in their roles.
- Supervision and management should help identify safeguarding, conduct, behaviour, and performance concerns at an early stage.
- Skills for Care safer employment guidance recommends regular supervision for all staff, at least once a month, with extra supervision where recruitment or vetting risk has been identified.
- Managers should discuss performance, values, behaviours, wellbeing, safeguarding, and conduct throughout the year, not only at an annual appraisal.
- Managers should record supervision and one-to-one notes, including concerns discussed, share them with the employee, and store them confidentially.
- Low-level concerns and "nagging doubts" should be identified, shared, and recorded at the earliest opportunity.
- Formal disciplinary, grievance, and investigation processes involving conduct or safeguarding concerns should comply with the Acas Code of Practice.
- Managers should be trained to carry out effective supervision and investigations with a safeguarding mindset.

How to apply in this project:
- Use supervision as early support and evidence, not as a disciplinary sanction.
- Use supervision records to show what was discussed, what support was offered, what standards were set, and whether concerns repeated.
- Escalate from supervision to fact-finding or formal investigation where concerns persist, involve safeguarding, affect safe care, or show conduct risk.
