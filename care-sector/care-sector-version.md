# Care-sector disciplinary version

## Purpose
This version adapts the disciplinary process for care homes, domiciliary care, supported living, and similar regulated care settings.

## Additional care-sector considerations
- safeguarding duties
- medication management
- accurate care records
- resident dignity and safety
- supervision and competence
- CQC reporting and incident handling
- interaction with whistleblowing and safeguarding policies
- Skills for Care guidance on supervision, safer employment, performance management, and identifying concerns

## Typical scenarios
- repeated lateness affecting handover
- failure to complete care notes
- medication documentation errors
- missed welfare checks
- inappropriate conduct toward residents or colleagues
- safeguarding concerns or breaches

## Process emphasis
- use welfare meetings early where the issue is minor and supportable
- record welfare meetings clearly where the issue involves health, stress, absence, disability, pregnancy, mental health, caring responsibilities, work impact, reasonable adjustments, or safe care impact
- use regular supervision to discuss performance, values, behaviour, wellbeing, safeguarding, and conduct
- record low-level concerns and patterns early, including "nagging doubts"
- escalate promptly where safety, dignity, or safeguarding are affected
- investigate carefully and independently
- document evidence and decisions clearly
- consider suspension only where necessary
- coordinate with safeguarding and regulatory processes where needed
- train managers to handle supervision, challenging conversations, and investigations with a safeguarding mindset

## Outcome guidance
- first warning for lower-level but repeated concerns
- final warning for serious or repeated concerns with continued risk
- dismissal only where justified by the facts, policy, and legal framework

## Handbook-aligned notes
The staff handbook used by the homes pack contains formal disciplinary, capability, appeal, whistleblowing, termination, record accuracy, and alcohol/drugs at work provisions. These should be treated as operationally relevant examples, especially for care settings where record integrity and resident safety matter.
