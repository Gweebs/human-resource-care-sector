# Human Resource Care Sector

This repository is a working HR disciplinary process pack for UK adult social care.

It is designed for practical use in care homes, domiciliary care, supported living, and similar regulated care settings. It brings together a fair UK disciplinary process with care-sector issues such as safeguarding, supervision, medication, records, resident dignity, CQC readiness, and Skills for Care guidance.

The aim is simple: help managers deal with staff concerns early, fairly, consistently, and with good records.

It should not be used as a shortcut around:

## Core Approach

The process follows a simple principle:

Support first where safe. Investigate where facts are unclear or risk is higher. Use formal action only where it is proportionate and fair.

The usual route is:

1. Welfare meeting or informal support
2. Record of conversation
3. Supervision
4. Matter of concern
5. Fact-finding
6. Investigation
7. Formal disciplinary meeting
8. Outcome
9. Appeal
10. Closure and follow-up

In care settings, some concerns need faster escalation. Examples include safeguarding, medication, falsified records, serious confidentiality breaches, alcohol or drug impairment at work, abuse, neglect, poor treatment, or conduct that may affect resident safety or dignity.

## Key Concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Record of conversation | An early management record. It records a discussion, expected standards, support offered, and review date. It is not a formal disciplinary warning. |
| Supervision | A regular recorded meeting or discussion between a manager and staff member to review work, conduct, performance, wellbeing, learning, competence, safeguarding, support needs, and actions. |
| Matter of concern | A concern that needs closer attention before deciding whether formal action is needed. It may lead to support, fact-finding, investigation, or no further action. |
| Fact-finding | A short evidence-gathering stage used to understand what happened and whether formal investigation is needed. |
| Investigation | A more formal evidence-gathering stage used where disciplinary action may be justified or facts are serious or disputed. |
| Disciplinary meeting | A formal meeting where the allegation, evidence, and employee response are considered before an outcome is decided. |
| Appeal | A route for the employee to challenge a formal outcome. |

## How To Use This Repository

### 1. For a live staff concern

Start with the route decision.

Ask:

- Is this welfare, conduct, capability, safeguarding, grievance, or whistleblowing?
- Is there an immediate resident safety issue?
- Is the concern minor, repeated, unclear, serious, or disputed?
- What evidence exists?
- Has support or supervision already happened?
- Is formal action proportionate?

Use:

- `process/decision-tree.md`
- `process/checklist.md`
- `care-sector/care-home-manager-quick-guide.md`
- `care-sector/care-sector-investigations-guide.md`

### 2. For a record of conversation

Use this where the concern is early, minor, unclear, or supportable.

Record:

- date and time
- people present
- factual concern
- staff response
- support offered
- standard expected
- action agreed
- review date
- whether the matter may escalate if repeated

Use:

- `figma/figma-disciplinary-process-guide.md`
- `skills/care-sector-hr-disciplinary/references/process.md`
- `skills/care-sector-hr-disciplinary/references/templates-and-outputs.md`

### 3. For supervision

Use supervision as regular recorded management support. It should review work practice, conduct, performance, wellbeing, learning, competence, safeguarding awareness, support needs, and agreed actions.

Supervision is not a disciplinary sanction.

Use supervision records to show:

- what was discussed
- what support was offered
- what standard was expected
- what action was agreed
- whether the concern repeated
- whether escalation was reasonable

Use:

- `care-sector/care-sector-version.md`
- `sources/skills-for-care-managing-people.md`
- `skills/care-sector-hr-disciplinary/references/care-sector.md`

### 4. For matters of concern

Use a matter of concern where the issue needs closer attention before formal action is considered.

This may be right where:

- facts are incomplete
- there is a repeated pattern
- informal support has not worked
- the issue may be conduct or capability
- care records, handover, medication, attendance, or conduct are affected
- a low-level concern needs recording and review

Use:

- `process/process-map.md`
- `process/decision-tree.md`
- `figma/figma-disciplinary-process-guide.md`

### 5. For investigations

Use an investigation where formal disciplinary action may be needed or facts are serious or disputed.

In care settings, check:

- resident safety
- dignity
- safeguarding
- medication records
- care notes
- incident reports
- handover records
- supervision notes
- training and competence
- earlier low-level concerns

Use:

- `care-sector/care-sector-investigations-guide.md`
- `process/process-map.md`
- `process/checklist.md`

## Common Escalation Thresholds

| Situation | Likely starting point |
|---|---|
| Minor first-time issue | Record of conversation, supervision, coaching |
| Repeated low-level issue | Matter of concern or supervision review |
| Unclear facts | Fact-finding |
| Serious or disputed facts | Investigation |
| Resident safety affected | Safeguarding check and prompt investigation |
| Medication issue | Incident review, competence check, possible investigation |
| Falsified records | Serious misconduct consideration |
| Alcohol or drug impairment at work | Immediate safety response and formal review |
| Skills or confidence issue | Capability, training, supervision |
| Protected disclosure | Whistleblowing route |
| Employee complaint | Grievance route |

## Drafting Style

The repository is written in a practical UK care-sector style:

- plain English
- active voice
- short sentences
- clear next steps
- no corporate gloss
- no overstatement
- facts separated from assumptions
- proportionate action
- clear records

When facts are missing, use this approach:

```text
What is known:
[facts]

What is not known:
[missing evidence]

Next step:
[records, witness account, supervision note, or meeting needed before deciding route]
```

## Quality Checks Before Using Any Draft

Before using a letter, note, policy, or guide from this repository, check:

- Is the allegation clear?
- Are the facts separated from assumptions?
- Has the employee had or will they have a fair chance to respond?
- Has welfare or support been considered?
- Is this conduct, capability, safeguarding, grievance, or whistleblowing?
- Is resident safety or dignity affected?
- Are care records, medication records, incident forms, and supervision notes checked?
- Is formal action proportionate?
- Is the appeal route included where there is a formal outcome?
- Does the wording match the staff handbook and contract?
